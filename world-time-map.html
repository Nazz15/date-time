What Is the World Time Map?

The World Time Map is an interactive visualization of the Earth's current time zones with a live day and night overlay. As the Earth rotates, the boundary between daylight and nighttime — called the solar terminator — moves westward at roughly 1,040 km per hour. This map updates in real time so you can always see which parts of the world are currently experiencing sunrise, daylight, sunset, or night.

Each point on the map can be clicked to reveal the exact local time, time zone name, UTC offset, and country flag for that location. You can also search for any city or country and pin multiple locations to compare their times simultaneously.

How Time Zones Work

The Earth is divided into 24 primary time zones, each roughly 15 degrees of longitude wide, corresponding to one hour of time difference. In practice, political and geographic factors mean real-world time zone boundaries are far more irregular — some countries span multiple zones, while others (like India, Iran, and Nepal) use half-hour or quarter-hour offsets to compromise between geography and national unity.

All time zones are defined relative to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), a precise atomic-clock standard that never changes for daylight saving. Most zones are expressed as UTC+ or UTC− offsets ranging from UTC−12 to UTC+14.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the day/night overlay?

The terminator line is calculated mathematically from the Sun's current position using the solar declination and equation of time, updated every 60 seconds. It accurately reflects where civil twilight begins and ends on Earth, though it represents the idealized terminator on a flat map rather than accounting for local terrain or atmospheric refraction.

What's the difference between UTC and GMT?

GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) is the mean solar time at the Prime Meridian in Greenwich, England. UTC is the modern atomic-clock-based standard that replaced GMT as the world's time reference in 1972. The numerical difference is usually zero, but UTC is more precisely defined — it's the actual standard your computer's clock synchronizes to.

Why do some countries use unusual UTC offsets like +5:30 or +9:30?

Most half-hour and quarter-hour offsets exist for political or geographic reasons. India uses UTC+5:30 because a one-hour boundary would either put its west coast too early or its east coast too late, and a single national time zone was seen as important for unity. Nepal uses UTC+5:45 — the only quarter-hour offset still in active use — as a deliberate distinction from neighboring India.

Which country spans the most time zones?

France spans the most time zones of any country (12, including overseas territories), followed by Russia (11) and the United States (11 including territories). Russia is the largest country by land area that spans multiple zones within its mainland, stretching from UTC+2 to UTC+12.
