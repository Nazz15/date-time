Best Time to Schedule an International Meeting (With Real Examples)

How to find the overlap window, the slots that work for common city combinations, and how to stop one team always taking the 10 PM call.

NNazz · SEO & time-zone nerd· Updated 26 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Start with each city's 'reasonable hours' (about 7:00–22:00), not just 9-to-5.
  • US East + UK: 9 AM–12 PM New York. US + India: US morning / Indian evening.
  • If no slot is fair, rotate it, and move status updates to async.

Quick answer: the best time for an international meeting is a slot that falls inside every participant's reasonable hours, roughly 7:00 AM to 10:00 PM local time. For US East Coast and UK teams, that's 9:00 AM–12:00 PM New York (2:00–5:00 PM London). For US and India teams, it's the US morning and Indian evening. When no slot is fair to everyone, rotate it.

Why meetings across time zones are hard

When your team sits in one city, the working day overlaps completely. Add London, Mumbai and New York, and that shared window shrinks to an hour or two, sometimes none at all. Then daylight saving moves it twice a year.

The usual result is that one group quietly absorbs the pain. In my experience it's often the team in India taking 9 PM calls week after week because "that's when the US is online". That works for a while, then it shows up as burnout and lower energy in exactly the meetings that matter.

Step 1: Find the overlap window

Write down each city's hours on one timeline, in UTC or in one reference city. Mark two bands for each person:

  • Core hours (roughly 9:00 AM–6:00 PM): ideal.
  • Reasonable hours (7:00–9:00 AM and 6:00–10:00 PM): acceptable occasionally.

The overlap window is where everyone's bands meet. If there's core-hour overlap, use it. If there's only reasonable-hour overlap, that's your slot, but keep those meetings short and infrequent.

Step 2: Rotate the inconvenient slot

If the only option is early for one region and late for another, don't pick one and leave it forever. Alternate. For a weekly US–India call, run it at 9:00 AM New York (7:30 PM India) one week and 8:00 PM New York (6:30 AM India) the next. Each side takes the awkward slot half the time.

Rotation feels like extra admin, but it signals something important: nobody's time is worth less because of where they live. Teams notice.

Step 3: Use async for everything else

The best way to make time zones painless is to have fewer live meetings. Keep real-time calls for decisions, debates and anything that needs a quick back-and-forth. Move status updates, demos and FYIs to async:

  • Written updates in a shared doc or channel, posted before the end of each person's day.
  • Short recorded videos instead of walk-through meetings.
  • Deadlines in UTC ("by 12:00 UTC Thursday") so nobody has to convert.

Five rules that keep international meetings fair

  1. Always send invites from a calendar, never as a time in an email. Calendars handle time zones and daylight saving automatically.
  2. Put two or three local times in the description, e.g. "9:00 AM New York / 2:00 PM London / 7:30 PM Mumbai".
  3. Avoid Friday afternoons in the US: it's already Saturday in Asia and Australia.
  4. Check local weekends and holidays. Not everyone works Monday to Friday.
  5. Recheck recurring meetings in March and November. The US and Europe change clocks on different dates, so the gap shifts for a week or two. Our clock change guide has the 2027 dates.
💡 Tip: when you invite people across several countries, recurring meetings should be anchored to one city's time. Everyone else's local time will shift when daylight saving changes, so warn the team a week ahead.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time for a meeting between the US and India?

For US East Coast and India, 8:30–10:00 AM New York time works best. That's 7:00–8:30 PM in India in winter and 6:00–7:30 PM in US summer. For the US West Coast, try 7:00–8:00 PM Pacific, which is 8:30–9:30 AM the next day in India in winter.

How many hours should time zones overlap for a team?

Two to four hours of shared working time is enough for most teams. That's sufficient for one or two meetings a day plus quick questions. Less than an hour usually means you need to lean heavily on async work.

Should meetings rotate between time zones?

Yes, for recurring meetings where there's no slot inside everyone's working hours. Alternating between two times, so each region takes the inconvenient slot every other week, is the simplest way to share the cost fairly.

What is the best time for a US and UK meeting?

Between 9:00 AM and 12:00 PM New York time, which is 2:00–5:00 PM in London. For the US West Coast, 8:00–9:00 AM Pacific (4:00–5:00 PM London) is the only overlap.

Which days should I avoid for international meetings?

Friday afternoons in the US are already Saturday in Asia and Australia, and Monday mornings in Asia are still Sunday in the Americas. Also check local weekends: in Saudi Arabia, for example, the weekend is Friday and Saturday.
