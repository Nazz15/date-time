How Does a World Clock Work?

Every location on Earth is assigned a time zone — an offset measured in hours (and sometimes half-hours) from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), the world's universal time standard. TimezoneBudy calculates each city's local time by applying its UTC offset to your device's real-time clock. Times automatically adjust for Daylight Saving Time (DST) wherever it applies, so you never have to manually account for seasonal clock changes.

The global time zone system divides the Earth into 24 primary zones, each roughly 15 degrees of longitude wide. In practice, political and geographic decisions mean real boundaries are far more varied — some countries like India (UTC+5:30) and Nepal (UTC+5:45) use non-standard half-hour offsets, while China spans five geographic time zones but uses a single national time.

UTC vs GMT — What's the Difference?

GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) is the mean solar time at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, England. UTC replaced it in 1972 as the world's primary time reference, using precise atomic clocks instead of astronomical observation. The two are numerically equivalent for everyday use — both sit at UTC/GMT+0 — but UTC is the modern scientific standard that your phone, computer, and every server on the internet synchronise to.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a World Clock?

A world clock is a tool that displays the current local time for multiple cities or countries simultaneously. Rather than converting manually, a world clock does the calculation automatically, accounting for each location's UTC offset and any active Daylight Saving Time adjustment. TimezoneBudy's world clock shows live times for 130+ cities updated every second.

How does a World Clock work?

A world clock works by taking a single reference time — Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) — and adding or subtracting each city's fixed UTC offset to calculate its local time. For example, London in winter is UTC+0, New York is UTC−5, and Mumbai is UTC+5:30. The clock updates in real time using your device's system clock, which is itself synchronised to global UTC servers. Daylight Saving Time adjustments are applied automatically based on each timezone's rules.

What is the difference between UTC and GMT?

GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) is a time zone based on the solar time at the Prime Meridian in Greenwich, England. UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is a modern atomic-clock-based time standard that replaced GMT in 1972 as the world's official reference point. For practical everyday purposes the two are the same — both are at offset zero — but UTC is the precise scientific standard used by computers, servers, and international systems worldwide. TimezoneBudy uses UTC as its underlying reference for all time calculations.

Why do different countries have different time zones?

Time zones exist because the Earth rotates 360 degrees every 24 hours, meaning the Sun's position — and therefore local noon — differs by roughly one hour for every 15 degrees of longitude. Without time zones, clocks in cities east and west of each other would show wildly different times for midday. The system of 24 primary time zones, each one hour apart, was standardised in the late 19th century to enable coordinated railway schedules, global communication, and international trade. Today, the exact boundaries are set by governments and often reflect political borders rather than strict geography.
